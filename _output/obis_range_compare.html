1 Why

obis_range_constraint.qmd scores the range rules A–E on one model run at a time (OBIS’s published models and, through obis_range_constraint_{og,ogm}.qmd, each of ours). This page puts the runs side by side: how good each model is, how many cells each rule keeps per US EEZ subregion under each run, where the regional effort test agrees, and what each run cost. It reads only the committed csvs under data/og/ (scripts/og/cv_metrics.R, scripts/og/timings.R and the scorecard notebooks write them), so it renders on any machine.

run fit points background layers species
mpaeu OBIS’s published models (their thinning, their tuning) random cells of the masked stack OBIS’s depth choice all seven
ogp pinned to OBIS’s fit points and hyperparameters (control) random surface leatherback, right whale
ogc our thinning, our tuning random surface leatherback, right whale
og our thinning, our tuning target group = the species’ ORDER (turtle records for turtles) surface six (loggerhead apart)
ogm our thinning, our tuning target group = air-breathing megafauna surface six (loggerhead running)

2 Model quality

The pipeline reports two sets of metrics per algorithm: the spatial-block cross-validation (cv, what its acceptance gate reads, CBI ≥ 0.3) and the independent evaluation records (eval, records held back before the fit; for these species only 11–24 presences, so a single eval AUC is noisy — read the pattern across runs, not one cell). The ensemble row is the mean over the accepted algorithms.

6 What this settles, and what it does not

  • The ORDER-level target-group background is not usable for these species; carry the megafauna (ogm) or random (ogc) background forward. The og scorecard stays as the record of why.
  • The acceptance gate (CV CBI ≥ 0.3) passed a model that is below chance on independent records; a floor on CV AUC or on the evaluation metrics would have caught it. That is a method change in the pipeline, not made.
  • Open (Ben): the loggerhead thinning (cap the fit points, pin OBIS’s fitocc, or a fixed thinning distance); which background to carry; the ratio = 0.25 guard and the 0.5° unit of the effort test; whether the leatherback’s P10 count of 51 vs 57 is acceptable.

```{r}
  select(common, region, acro, verdict) |> pivot_wider(names_from = acro, values_from = verdict) |> arrange(common, region)
kable(v |> select(common, region, any_of(names(acros))))
agree <- v |> filter(!is.na(og), !is.na(ogm)) |> summarise(n = n(), same = sum(og == ogm))
```

`og` and `ogm` reach the same verdict in `r agree$same` of `r agree$n` species × subregion cells; the test
reads records and effort, not the model, so a background change moves it only through the P10 footprint it
is applied to.

Maxent is ~91–95 % of a fit and roughly linear in fit points (5.5 k → 3 h 51, 8.5 k → 6 h 20, 24 k →

grid prune at a different resolution.

## What this settles, and what it does not

- The ORDER-level target-group background is not usable for these species; carry the megafauna (`ogm`) or
  random (`ogc`) background forward. The `og` scorecard stays as the record of why.
- The acceptance gate (CV CBI ≥ 0.3) passed a model that is below chance on independent records; a floor on
  CV AUC or on the evaluation metrics would have caught it. That is a method change in the pipeline, not made.
- Open (Ben): the loggerhead thinning (cap the fit points, pin OBIS's `fitocc`, or a fixed thinning
  distance); which background to carry; the `ratio = 0.25` guard and the 0.5° unit of the effort test;
  whether the leatherback's P10 count of 51 vs 57 is acceptable.
